summary: 'Melika is an architect with an unusual eye for detail, and a habit of asking what a space will feel like for the people who end up inside it. She was doing that work for studios and independent architects without a page of her own. This is the one-page site that gives her one.'
role: 'Everything. The design, the writing structure, the build, and the domain and hosting it runs on. A site this size does not need a team, and splitting it across one would have cost more in handovers than in work.'
problem: 'An architect who freelances is found by studios, by independent architects, and by people who saw one image somewhere and want to know who made it. Melika''s only address was an Instagram handle. That is fine for showing a piece of work and poor at everything after it: there is no place to say what she does, no way to read her history in order, and no obvious way to write to her that isn''t a message in a queue with everyone else''s. The gap was doing real damage to a specific claim. She tells clients their idea is good and illegible. A person who says that for a living, and then has nothing legible of her own to point at, is arguing against themselves before the conversation starts.'
built:
    - 'One page, in the order somebody actually reads: who she is, what she has been working on, and how to reach her. Nothing to navigate, because a portfolio this size does not need navigation, it needs an order.'
    - 'Her positioning in the first screen, in her own words rather than a job title. A visitor knows within one line whether she is the person they need.'
    - 'A dated history of the work, so a studio can see the shape of a career instead of a list of skills. The entries carry the client and the kind of work, which is what a person deciding whether to hire her is looking for.'
    - 'Email and phone in plain text at the top and at the foot, so getting in touch takes one tap and does not depend on an app.'
    - 'Built to open fast on a phone, because most people who find an architect find them on one, frequently on bad signal.'
